# CHAIN REACTION — CIVILIZATION GROWTH FROM EMPTY LAND

Status: canonical progression design for Meta6
Date: 2026-09-30

## 1. Purpose

This document defines how Chain Reaction grows from lifeless land into a complex medieval civilization.

The world must not begin as a finished city.

It begins with:
- terrain;
- water;
- forest;
- stone;
- fertile soil;
- dangerous or barren zones;
- a small number of RED OBSERVERS.

The player is an Observer from the Institute of Historical Observation.

The player does not directly choose a finished political system, culture, economy, or history.

The player places causes.

The world creates consequences.

Core progression:

EMPTY LAND
→ RESOURCES
→ FIRST INFRASTRUCTURE
→ FIRST SETTLERS
→ SETTLEMENT
→ INSTITUTIONS
→ POWER
→ KNOWLEDGE
→ INEQUALITY
→ CRISIS
→ HISTORICAL PERSONALITIES
→ OBSERVER INTERVENTION
→ UNINTENDED CONSEQUENCES
→ NEW HISTORICAL ORDER

There is no final static city state.

Every stable order can become the cause of the next historical phase.

---

## 2. Stage Zero — Empty Land

The map contains no city, ruler, organized economy, or permanent population.

Only geography exists.

Possible natural features:
- river;
- lake;
- forest;
- fertile plain;
- marsh;
- stone deposit;
- iron deposit;
- hills;
- cliffs;
- coast;
- dangerous crossing;
- old road or abandoned route.

The first visible special entities are RED OBSERVERS.

Their task is not "build a city instantly".

Their task is to create conditions that attract people.

Early glyphs:
- WELL;
- ROAD;
- STOREHOUSE;
- MILL;
- BRIDGE;
- FARM;
- MARKET;
- FORTIFIED YARD.

Example causal chain:

WELL
→ travellers stop
→ first families remain
→ informal camp
→ village possibility.

MARKET added near the well
→ merchants arrive
→ regular trade
→ wealth
→ theft
→ demand for guards
→ first political authority.

The first player lesson:

A useful object is also a cause of future conflict.

---

## 3. Stage One — First Settlers

Population begins with concrete social groups, not only a number.

Possible arrivals:
- farmers;
- carpenter;
- smith;
- trader;
- healer;
- refugees;
- labourers;
- beggars;
- hunters;
- religious wanderer;
- escaped criminal;
- former soldier.

The first settlement character emerges from infrastructure.

### Development directions

FARMS
→ agricultural settlement.

MARKET
→ trade town.

WALL / FORT
→ military settlement.

MONASTERY
→ religious centre.

SCHOOL
→ knowledge centre.

MINE
→ extraction economy.

PORT
→ trade, migration and smuggling hub.

These are tendencies, not fixed classes.

The settlement can later change direction.

---

## 4. Stage Two — Emergence of Authority

When the settlement grows, coordination becomes necessary.

Authority must emerge from conditions instead of being selected from a menu.

Possible structures:
- village elder;
- guild council;
- merchant council;
- noble house;
- military commander;
- religious authority;
- town magistrate;
- hereditary ruler.

Example emergence rules:

MARKET + WEALTHY MERCHANTS + WEAK MILITARY
→ merchant council.

WALL + WAR + STRONG GUARD
→ military ruler.

MONASTERY + FAMINE RELIEF
→ religious authority gains legitimacy.

LARGE ESTATES + ARMED RETAINERS
→ aristocratic rule.

The same city may move between systems over time.

---

## 5. Stage Three — Social Stratification

Growth creates visible differences.

The city starts separating into:
- noble district;
- merchant quarter;
- artisan streets;
- market;
- barracks;
- monastery district;
- poor district;
- refugee camp;
- criminal quarter;
- outer farms.

Core causal chains:

WEALTH
→ taxation
→ corruption
→ resentment.

MIGRATION
→ cheap labour
→ fast construction
→ overcrowding
→ disease.

WAR
→ refugees
→ housing shortage
→ crime
→ stronger guard
→ repression.

TRADE
→ wealth concentration
→ new elites
→ political competition.

No resource increase is purely positive.

---

## 6. Stage Four — Knowledge Appears

Knowledge is one of the main historical accelerators.

Possible chain:

HEALER
→ SCHOOL
→ LIBRARY
→ SCHOLAR
→ WORKSHOP
→ PRINTING HOUSE.

Knowledge creates both capability and conflict.

Example:

DISEASE
→ scholar studies water
→ evidence against sacred well
→ conflict with religious authority
→ accusation of heresy
→ persecution
→ underground knowledge network.

The Observer may know the correct scientific answer.

Giving that answer directly can expose the Observer and create a historical anomaly.

This is a core game tension.

---

## 7. Stage Five — First Major Crisis

The world begins to push back.

Major crisis types:
- famine;
- fire;
- plague;
- siege;
- riot;
- religious conflict;
- assassination;
- succession crisis;
- crop failure;
- flood;
- civil war.

Crises should emerge from prior conditions whenever possible.

Example:

POPULATION GROWTH
→ dense housing
→ polluted water
→ disease
→ overcrowded healer
→ rumours
→ scapegoating
→ violence.

The Observer may intervene at multiple points.

Late intervention must usually be more expensive and more visible.

---

## 8. Stage Six — Historical Personalities

At higher complexity, individual people become causal hubs.

Possible key roles:
- scholar;
- guard captain;
- merchant;
- priest;
- healer;
- printer;
- rebel;
- royal heir;
- judge;
- inventor;
- informer.

A character can create a long historical chain.

Example:

SCRIBE
→ discovers corruption
→ writes satire
→ text spreads
→ government bans it
→ printer copies it secretly
→ arrests
→ demonstrations
→ revolt.

The player may have caused this chain many turns earlier simply by creating a SCHOOL.

---

## 9. Stage Seven — Observer Exposure

At the start, the RED OBSERVERS are effectively invisible to history.

Repeated intervention changes this.

People begin noticing anomalies:
- an Observer appears before important events;
- unusual medicine works too well;
- an Observer survives impossible situations;
- strange devices are found;
- witnesses compare stories.

Introduce:

OBSERVER SUSPICION
OBSERVER EXPOSURE
OBSERVER EVIDENCE

Possible causal chain:

RUMOUR
→ witness
→ investigation
→ surveillance
→ safe house discovered
→ Observer network compromised.

Observers can:
- be followed;
- arrested;
- wounded;
- exposed;
- tortured;
- rescued;
- killed.

Observer death does not reset the world.

The history continues.

---

## 10. Six Major Civilization Directions

The same empty land can evolve toward very different historical structures.

### A. Trade City

MARKET
→ merchants
→ wealth
→ banking-like institutions
→ merchant council
→ family competition
→ corruption or republic.

Possible later transformations:
- oligarchy;
- republic;
- commercial empire;
- civil conflict.

### B. Fortress State

WAR
→ walls
→ army
→ military ruler
→ expansion
→ taxation
→ dictatorship or empire.

Possible later transformations:
- imperial expansion;
- military collapse;
- rebellion;
- permanent emergency rule.

### C. Religious City

MONASTERY
→ pilgrims
→ wealth
→ influence
→ moral authority
→ heresy conflict
→ religious rule.

Possible later transformations:
- theocracy;
- reform movement;
- schism;
- persecution.

### D. City of Knowledge

SCHOOL
→ library
→ scholars
→ experiments
→ new technology
→ conflict with old institutions.

Possible later transformations:
- intellectual republic;
- reformist monarchy;
- persecution of scholars;
- technological acceleration.

### E. Revolutionary City

POVERTY
→ repression
→ underground groups
→ revolt
→ victory
→ conflict among victors.

Possible later transformations:
- republic;
- terror;
- military takeover;
- restoration of monarchy.

### F. Observer-Contaminated City

FREQUENT INTERVENTION
→ rapid progress
→ unexplained anomalies
→ rumours
→ investigation
→ discovery of hidden technology.

Possible later transformations:
- cult of the Observers;
- hunt for Observers;
- technological leap;
- collapse after dependence on intervention.

## 11. Cross-Branch Transformation

Civilization directions must not be permanent classes.

Examples:

CITY OF KNOWLEDGE
→ war
→ emergency rule
→ military dictatorship.

TRADE REPUBLIC
→ plague
→ monastery controls relief
→ religious government.

REVOLUTIONARY CITY
→ civil war
→ general seizes power
→ new monarchy.

FORTRESS STATE
→ long peace
→ merchants grow powerful
→ commercial republic.

The interesting history is in transitions.

---

## 12. Long-Term Consequence Memory

The simulation must preserve causal memory across long periods.

A decision made very early can matter much later.

Example:

TURN 4
Observer secretly saves a child.

TURN 31
The child becomes an officer.

TURN 67
The officer suppresses a revolt.

TURN 102
The officer's son becomes ruler.

The system should be able to trace the later event back to the original intervention.

Required causal metadata:
- cause_event_id;
- parent_event_ids;
- actor lineage;
- institution lineage;
- place lineage;
- Observer intervention lineage.

The player must be able to discover:

"This exists because of something I did fifty events ago."

This is one of the core emotional rewards of Chain Reaction.

---

## 13. Event Narrative Standard

Ordinary event reports follow the canonical Meta6 rule:

ONE paragraph.
Approximately 35–55 words.

Structure:

WHAT HAPPENED
→ WORLD REACTION
→ POSSIBLE CONSEQUENCE.

Example:

"The first well attracted three families and a blacksmith. By the end of the week traders had begun stopping beside it, and someone was already charging strangers for water. If trade continues, a village will form here. If nobody limits the toll, its first institution may be extortion."

Longer prose is reserved for rare historical turning points.

---

## 14. Observer Decisions

The Observer should rarely receive a simple "good / bad" choice.

Instead:

OBSERVE
INTERVENE
CONCEAL
RESCUE
EVACUATE
INTRODUCE KNOWLEDGE
INFLUENCE A PERSON
REFUSE TO INTERVENE
BREAK PROTOCOL

Each action should alter:
- local history;
- faction power;
- suspicion;
- future opportunities;
- lives saved/lost;
- historical divergence.

The Institute may warn.

The Institute must not define the morally correct answer.

---

## 15. Persistent Death and Succession

Important people die.

Observers die.

Rulers die.

Scholars die.

The simulation continues.

When an Observer dies:
- death location persists;
- witnesses remember;
- equipment may remain;
- rumours may spread;
- factions may react;
- another Observer may later arrive.

A replacement Observer enters the same historical world, not a reset version.

This makes death part of history instead of a failure screen.

---

## 16. Player Experience Goal

The player should initially think:

"I am building a settlement."

Later:

"I am influencing a society."

Later:

"I am trying to understand a history that has become larger than my intentions."

And eventually:

"Something happening now exists because of a choice I made long ago and barely remembered."

That progression is the emotional spine of the game.

## 18. MVP Progression Slice

The first playable proof should demonstrate the entire philosophy in miniature.

### Start

- empty medieval terrain;
- one red Observer;
- no population.

### Player actions

1. Place WELL.
2. Place MARKET.
3. Place SCHOOL.

### Emergent sequence

WELL
→ settlers arrive.

MARKET
→ trade begins.

SCHOOL
→ scholar appears.

Scholar identifies a dangerous local problem.

Authority reacts.

Scholar becomes threatened.

Observer gets a choice:
- intervene;
- do nothing.

### Required outcomes

Both choices:
- alter later events;
- produce a 35–55 word narrative report;
- affect Observer suspicion;
- persist in causal history.

The world must visibly change from empty land into the beginning of a medieval settlement.

This MVP is successful only if the player can see that one simple construction decision begins a chain of human and political consequences.
